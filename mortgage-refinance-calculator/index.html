🏡 Mortgage Refinance Calculator

Rate-and-term refinance · monthly payment & interest comparison · break-even months

📘 About this tool

Refinancing means replacing your current mortgage with a new one, usually to lock in a lower interest rate (a rate-and-term refinance). A lower rate cuts your monthly principal-and-interest payment and the total interest you will pay — but only after you recover the closing costs, which in the U.S. typically run 2–5% of the loan amount. This calculator puts both loans side by side and answers the two questions that matter most: how much will I save each month, and how many months until the savings pay for the refinance?

Enter your current balance, rate, and remaining years, then the new rate, term, and estimated closing costs. The tool computes each monthly payment with the standard amortization formula, shows your monthly savings, your break-even point (closing costs ÷ monthly savings), and the interest you would pay over the rest of your current loan versus the new loan. The verdict line tells you plainly whether the numbers justify refinancing.

Everything is calculated locally in your browser — no account, no loan data uploaded, nothing tracked. Remember that a lower payment is not the whole story: taxes, insurance, and escrow are separate, and extending the term means more years of payments even at a lower rate.

⚠️ Disclaimer: Estimates for planning only — not tax or financial advice. Rates and rules change, so verify with your state or a qualified professional before filing or making big decisions.

📉 When does refinancing make sense?
Generally when the new rate is at least 0.5–1 percentage point lower and you plan to stay in the home longer than the break-even period. If you expect to move sooner than break-even, the closing costs usually make it a loss.

🧾 What counts as closing costs?
Origination and lender fees, appraisal, title insurance, recording fees, and points — commonly $4,000–$10,000 on a typical refinance. Your lender gives you a Loan Estimate with the real numbers before you commit.

⏱️ What is the break-even point?
It is the number of months until your monthly savings add up to the closing costs: closing costs ÷ monthly savings. If that is, say, 18 months, the refinance only “wins” if you keep the loan past month 18.

🔄 Should I keep the same term or restart at 30 years?
Restarting at 30 years lowers the payment but stretches interest over more years, and you may pay more total interest than the old loan. Compare “total interest” here — many homeowners refinance to a shorter term, like 15 years, to pay less interest overall.

🏦 What about taxes, insurance, and escrow?
This calculator covers principal and interest only. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, and PMI are usually held in escrow and rarely change because of a refinance, so they do not affect the comparison much.

⚠️ Is this financial advice?
No. Rates move daily and your credit score, equity, and loan program determine the rate you actually get. Verify quotes with a lender and read the Loan Estimate carefully before closing.

📐 Quick formulas
Monthly payment M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ), where P = balance, r = monthly rate, n = months
Monthly savings = old payment − new payment
Break-even = closing costs ÷ monthly savings
Total interest over a loan = (payment × months) − principal
