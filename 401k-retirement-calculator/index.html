📈 401(k) Retirement Calculator

Your contributions + employer match + compound growth · project your balance

📘 About this tool

A 401(k) is the most powerful retirement account most American workers have: contributions go in pre-tax (or post-tax into a Roth 401(k)), investment gains grow tax-deferred, and many employers add free matching money on top. This calculator projects how much your 401(k) could be worth by retirement when you combine your own contributions, an employer match, salary growth, and compound investment returns.

It runs a year-by-year simulation from your current age to your planned retirement age: each year the balance earns your expected return, your contribution is added (capped at the IRS annual limit, with the age-50 catch-up added automatically), and your employer's match is credited up to the plan's cap. The result shows your projected balance, how much of it came from you versus your employer versus investment growth, and roughly what that could mean as monthly income using the common 4% withdrawal guideline.

Everything is calculated locally in your browser — no account, no login, no data uploaded. The result is an estimate, not a guarantee: returns are never smooth, plans differ in match formulas, and you may change jobs, contribution rates, or markets along the way. Re-run it whenever your salary or contribution changes.

💰 How much should I contribute?
At minimum, contribute enough to get the full employer match — that is free money. A common goal is 15% of income including the match. This tool lets you test a dollar amount or a percentage of salary.

🏦 What is the 2026 contribution limit?
The IRS elective deferral limit for 2026 is $24,000, plus a $7,500 catch-up if you are 50 or older (workers 60–63 may contribute up to $11,250 in catch-up). Limits rise most years — verify the current numbers at IRS.gov, and edit the fields if yours differ.

🤝 How does an employer match usually work?
A typical formula is “50% of your contributions up to 6% of salary,” meaning the employer adds $0.50 for every $1 you put in until your contributions reach 6% of pay. Many plans also have a vesting schedule — unvested match money may be forfeited if you leave early.

📉 What return should I use?
7% is a common long-term average for a diversified stock-heavy portfolio before inflation (about 4–5% after inflation). Try 5% for a conservative case and 9% for an aggressive one — the range shows how sensitive the future is to returns.

🏝️ What will my balance actually buy in retirement?
The 4% rule suggests you can withdraw about 4% of your balance in year one (rising with inflation) with a high chance of not outliving the money. That is a planning shorthand, not financial advice.

⚠️ Is this financial advice?
No. It is a simplified planning estimate. Fees, taxes on withdrawals, early-withdrawal rules, and your full retirement picture matter — talk to a plan advisor or fee-only professional before big decisions.

📐 Quick formulas
Yearly growth = balance × expected return
Your contribution = min(contribution, IRS limit + catch-up if 50+)
Employer match = min(contribution, salary × match cap) × match percent
4% rule income = projected balance × 4% ÷ 12 (monthly)
