🏠 Rent vs Buy & Home Affordability

Compare renting vs buying · Break-even year · How much house you can afford

📘 About this tool

Should you keep renting or buy a home? The honest answer depends on how long you will stay, the price you pay, and what your money could earn elsewhere. This tool models both paths side by side: as a buyer you pay a down payment, mortgage principal and interest, property tax, insurance, maintenance, and HOA dues while building equity; as a renter you pay rent that grows each year and invest the money you did not spend. It then shows which path leaves you ahead at your chosen time horizon and in which year buying starts to win.

The second section answers the other classic question: how much home can I afford? Using your income, other debts, down payment, and typical lender caps (a housing payment near 28% of gross income and total debts near 36%), it back-solves a realistic maximum price — including property tax and insurance — so you can search listings with a number, not a guess.

All figures are editable assumptions and all math runs locally in your browser. Home prices, rates, taxes, and rents vary by city; update the defaults to match your market.

⚠️ Disclaimer: Estimates for planning only — not tax or financial advice. Rates and rules change, so verify with your state or a qualified professional before filing or making big decisions.

📏 How long should I plan to stay before buying?
Buying usually wins after roughly 5–7 years because closing costs and the down payment take years to be recovered through equity and price growth. Under 3–5 years, renting is often cheaper once selling costs are included.

🏦 What is included in the mortgage payment?
Principal and interest (P&I) plus the property tax, insurance, maintenance, and HOA portions you enter. Property tax and maintenance are modeled as percentages of the home's current value.

📈 Why does the renter's invested money grow?
Because the down payment and any monthly savings from renting are assumed to earn an investment return. If you would not actually invest that money, set the return to 0%.

💵 How is "affordable" defined?
Lenders typically cap the housing payment at ~28% of gross monthly income and total monthly debts at ~36%. The tool uses the stricter of the two caps for your numbers.

⚠️ Is this financial advice?
No — it is a transparent estimate. Local taxes, insurance markets, mortgage fees, and lifestyle costs vary widely.

📐 Quick formulas
Monthly P&I = P × r(1+r)ⁿ ÷ ((1+r)ⁿ − 1), r = APR÷12, n = months
Buyer net worth at year Y = home value − mortgage balance remaining
Renter net worth = invested down payment + monthly savings, compounded
Break-even year = first year buyer net worth ≥ renter net worth
