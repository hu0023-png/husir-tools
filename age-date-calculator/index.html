🎂 Age & Date Calculator

Exact age · days between dates · add or subtract time — runs 100% in your browser

📘 About this tool

Three date questions answered instantly, with US-style output (month/day/year). From a date of birth, get the exact age in years, months, and days plus the total days lived and days until the next birthday. Between any two dates, get the total number of days and the breakdown into years, months, and days — useful for pregnancy due dates, leases, warranties, subscriptions, and milestones. Finally, add or subtract years, months, or days from a date to find a deadline or anniversary, with the result’s weekday included.

Dates are handled in your local timezone and leap years are accounted for automatically, so February 29 birthdays and leap-day intervals behave correctly. Everything runs entirely on your device — no account, no upload, fully offline.

📅 How do you count the days between two dates?
As elapsed 24-hour periods. “Between” a Monday and the following Monday is 7 days. If you need a count that includes both endpoints, add 1 to the result.

🎃 What happens to February 29 birthdays?
In a non-leap year the birthday falls on February 28 or March 1, depending on convention. This tool counts the calendar day that exists; February 29 is used only in leap years.

💭 Why does age in years/months/days not always add up neatly?
Months have different lengths, so the breakdown is calendar-aware: it borrows days from the previous month (and months from the previous year) exactly like counting age on paper.

🕐 Which timezone is used?
Your local timezone. Date-only inputs are treated as local midnight, so results match what you see on a wall calendar.

🔒 Is any date sent to a server?
Never. All calculations happen locally in your browser with zero network calls.

📐 Quick reference
Leap year = divisible by 4, except centuries unless divisible by 400 (2000 and 2024 are leap; 1900 and 2100 are not)
Average year = 365.2425 days  ·  months: 28/29/30/31 days
Age = calendar difference from date of birth to the reference date (days first, borrowing as needed)
