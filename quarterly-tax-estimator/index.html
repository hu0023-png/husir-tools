🧾 Quarterly Estimated Tax Calculator

1099 & self-employed · SE tax + federal income tax · 1040-ES quarterly payments

📘 About this tool

If you earn 1099 income — freelancing, consulting, a side business, or gig work — nobody withholds taxes for you. The IRS expects you to pay estimated tax four times a year on Form 1040-ES. This calculator estimates what those payments should be: it applies the self-employment tax (Social Security + Medicare) to your net business income, subtracts the standard deduction and the deductible half of SE tax, applies the 2026 federal income tax brackets for your filing status, and splits the result into four equal quarterly payments with their due dates.

Everything runs locally in your browser — no account, no upload, no Social Security number. Treat the result as a planning estimate, not a filing instruction: your real liability depends on business expenses, itemized deductions, credits, and state taxes, which are separate. If your income arrives unevenly through the year, the IRS annualized income installment method can lower earlier payments.

🧑‍💻 Who should pay quarterly estimated tax?
Anyone who expects to owe at least $1,000 in federal tax for the year and has no employer withholding, or has withholding that covers less than 90% of the year's tax.

🧮 How is self-employment tax calculated?
SE tax applies to 92.35% of your net profit at 15.3%: 12.4% Social Security (up to the wage base) plus 2.9% Medicare. Half of that SE tax is deductible on your income tax return.

📅 What are the quarterly due dates?
April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15 of the following year. If a date falls on a weekend or holiday, the deadline moves to the next business day.

💸 What if my income changes mid-year?
Re-run this calculator with an updated estimate. Paying more in later quarters is allowed, and the annualized income method may waive penalties if income was genuinely back-loaded.

🏛️ What about state taxes?
Most states with an income tax also require quarterly payments — usually a separate percentage of the same income. Check your state's revenue department; this tool covers federal only.

⚠️ Is this tax advice?
No. It is a simplified estimate using 2026 rates. Verify with the Form 1040-ES worksheet, IRS Publication 505, or a tax professional.

📐 Quick formulas
SE tax = net business income × 92.35% × 15.3%
Taxable income = net + other income − ½ SE tax − standard deduction
Quarterly payment = (federal income tax + SE tax − already paid) ÷ 4
2026 standard deduction: $15,000 single · $30,000 married · $22,500 head of household
