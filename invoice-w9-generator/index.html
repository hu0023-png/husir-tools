🧾 Invoice & W-9 Generator

Freelance invoice · W-9 info worksheet · print or save as PDF · nothing leaves your device

📘 About this tool

Two small chores every freelancer and small business hits: sending a clean invoice after the work is done, and asking a US contractor for their W-9 before paying them. This page does both locally. Build an itemized invoice with your business details, line items, discount and tax, then print it or save it as a PDF with your browser — no account, no upload, no watermark. The W-9 tab formats the information a payee gives you (name, tax classification, address, TIN fields, signature) on a printable worksheet that mirrors the official form's layout.

Why bother? In the US, when you pay an independent contractor $600 or more in a calendar year, you generally need their name and taxpayer ID to file a 1099-NEC — and the IRS wants that info collected before the first payment. Keep a signed copy for your records.

🧾 What is the difference between an invoice and a receipt?
An invoice is a request for payment sent before or at the time of work; a receipt proves payment was made. Use the receipt generator on this site for receipts, and this page for invoices.

📄 Who needs a W-9 from me?
Any US business that pays you $600 or more a year as an independent contractor will ask for one before paying. If you are the business, collect a W-9 from each contractor before their first payment.

🔢 What is a TIN?
Your Taxpayer Identification Number: a Social Security number (SSN) for individuals or an Employer Identification Number (EIN) for businesses.

📅 When is a 1099 required?
Generally when payments to one contractor total $600 or more in a calendar year — most platforms and clients will ask you to fill a W-9 so they can issue the form in January.

🔒 Is my financial data uploaded?
No. Everything is typed, formatted, and printed inside your browser and never sent anywhere.

📐 Quick formulas
Line amount = quantity × rate.
Subtotal = Σ line amounts; taxable = subtotal − discount.
Tax = taxable × tax rate; total = taxable + tax.
1099 threshold ≈ $600 per contractor per calendar year.
