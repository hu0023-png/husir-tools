🐾 Pet Age Calculator

Dog & cat age in human years · life stage · full age chart

📘 About this tool

“Dog years” is everyone's favorite party fact, but the old “multiply by 7” rule badly overestimates young pets and underestimates seniors. The widely used modern guideline is the 15/9/+4–6 method: most dogs and cats reach about 15 human years by their first birthday, about 24 by their second, and then age 4 human years per year after that — with larger dog breeds aging faster than small ones once they are past the puppy stage.

This calculator applies that guideline to your pet's exact age in years and months. Pick dog or cat (and a size for dogs), enter the age, and you get the human-year equivalent, a life-stage label (puppy, adolescent, adult, or senior for dogs; kitten, adult, or senior for cats), and a full comparison chart up to age 15–20 so you can see how the numbers climb. Knowing a pet's rough “human” age helps owners notice when joints, teeth, and energy levels start to change and when senior care, diet, or more frequent vet checkups deserve attention.

Nothing here is medical advice and no single chart is perfect — size, breed, genetics, and lifestyle all matter. The numbers are a friendly, useful frame for understanding your pet, not a diagnosis. Everything runs locally in your browser with no signup and no data uploaded.

🐕 Why is “1 dog year = 7 human years” wrong?
Pets mature much faster than humans early in life — a 1-year-old dog is roughly a 15-year-old human, not a 7-year-old. Dogs also slow down as they age (large breeds especially), so a flat 7× never fits the real curve.

📏 Why does dog size matter?
Large breeds age faster as adults: a Great Dane is usually senior by 8, while a Chihuahua may not be senior until 11 or 12. Small dogs often live into their mid-teens; giant breeds rarely do. The size options here use the common small/medium/large split.

🐈 Do cats age the same way as dogs?
Very close in the first two years (1 year ≈ 15 human, 2 years ≈ 24). After that, most charts use +4 human years per cat year — a 10-year-old cat is roughly 56 and considered mature-to-senior.

🔁 Why is the age different between months and years?
A 6-month-old kitten is already halfway through its “first 15 human years,” so the calculator fills in between the yearly marks instead of rounding. That is why a 1.5-year-old dog shows roughly 19–20 rather than just “15.”

🩺 When should I treat my pet as a senior?
Common vet guidance: cats and small dogs around age 10–11, medium dogs around 10, and large/giant breeds around 7–8. Senior pets benefit from yearly (or twice-yearly) checkups, blood work, dental care, and joint-friendly exercise.

🧬 How accurate is any pet-age chart?
Charts are population averages, not per-pet science. Breed, weight, diet, and luck all change the real answer — treat this as a starting point and let your vet set the care plan.

📐 Quick formulas (15/9/+4–6 guideline)
First year = 15 human years · Second year = +9 (so age 2 = 24)
Each year after: dogs add 4 (small) / 5 (medium) / 6 (large), cats add 4
Example: 6-year-old small dog = 24 + (6 − 2) × 4 = 40 human years
